# Gameplay loops and player retention

## Loops at four scales

| Scale | Length | What it is | Example in a sandbox |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action | seconds | input, response, result | swing, block breaks, item pops out |
| Goal | 5 to 15 minutes | a plan carried out | a mining trip, a house, a boss attempt |
| Session | an evening | a milestone reached | iron gear, a first vehicle, a new place |
| Arc | weeks | the long story of a world | all bosses down, a city, a mod made |

Each loop should feed the next: actions give materials, materials fund goals, goals unlock
milestones, milestones open new goals. Check each loop alone (the *isolation test*): if the action
loop is not pleasant to do for its own sake, no reward structure will save it.

**Loops and arcs (Daniel Cook).** A *loop* is something the player repeats and gets better at; an
*arc* is something experienced once (a story beat, a reveal). Loops last; arcs are spent. Big games
need both: loops for the hours, arcs for the memories. A *skill atom* is the smallest loop: action,
simulation, feedback, the player's updated model. Map the atoms of a feature to find which one is
broken.

**A good loop has:**
- a reason to begin (a need, a goal, curiosity),
- a decision (where to go, what to build, which tool),
- a risk or cost,
- quick, clear feedback,
- a reward that is useful in the next loop (not just a number),
- a natural end point so the player can stop happy, and a hook that makes them want to start again.

## Retention

Retention is measured as the share of new players who return after one day (D1), a week (D7) and
a month (D30). Each depends on something different:

- **D1 depends on the first session**: the core loop, the first ten minutes, performance. Players
  who are confused, bored or stuck in the first minutes do not return. Get them doing the fun thing
  fast; teach as they go.
- **D7 depends on goals**: a visible next thing to reach, a place they want to see, a build they
  want to finish, a reason the world is different each day.
- **D30 depends on depth and ownership**: systems still to master, their own creations, community,
  new content.

### What brings players back (in a game without a server)

- An unfinished plan: end a session with a goal in sight ("one more trip and I can make the anvil").
- A world that changes while away or each day: weather, crops, events, a daily seed challenge.
- Ownership: a home, pets, vehicles, a mod of their own.
- Mastery: something they could not do yesterday.
- Curiosity: a locked door, an unexplored place marked on the map, a rumour in the journal.

### What drives players away

- Not knowing what to do next.
- Losing a lot of progress to something they could not see coming.
- Repetition with nothing new to learn (grind).
- Clutter: too many systems introduced at once.
- Waiting with nothing to do.
- Bugs, crashes, lost saves (the worst of all).

## Ethics of retention

Make players want to come back, never feel they *have* to. Test: if the player stopped for a month,
would they come back happy or anxious? Avoid streaks that punish missing a day, timers that exist
to pressure, and fear of missing out. A daily challenge is fine; a daily login that takes something
away if missed is not.

## Session design

- Let a session end at many points: saves anywhere, short goals, a safe place.
- Mix task lengths so a short session still achieves something.
- Front-load the reward: the first minute of a session should give something (crops grown, a
  storm passed, the journal's next lead).
